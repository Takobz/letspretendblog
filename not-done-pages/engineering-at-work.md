I Witnessed Engineering At Work

## Just A Short Background.

As it per usual in the world of software moves at rapid pace, and I have been struggling to keep up.
So I sort of lost the motivation to try learning, yeah it is a weird time in my career.

While this is happening I have been battling with trying to remind myself why I love software developement.
In the search I came across a book "Mordern Software Development" by David Farley, in there was a statement that stuck with me.
It went something  "Engineering is using scientific methods to solve problems, in a scaleable and economy friendly way, and we need to experts at":
 - Learning,
 - Handling Complexity.
 
My mind was fixated at learning and learning at pace, which involves iterative learning and a fast feedback loop, i.e experimentation.
The book made me feel good about my half baked solutions that showed I had assumptions and needed to pivot or good deeper into a concept.

## Why Am I Even Writing This
Well at my work place a lot of teams were jumping on trying out using AI Tools to accelarate their Software Delivery LifeCycle.
While most of us were happy to see code being generated at a blink of an eye, one team did something different, the applied science to the AI Hype.

This inspired me a lot, it spark a fire in me, and all that "new tools to learn" didn't become a blocker or as scary, and I'd like to talk about that.


## The Problem They Had.
So the team wanted to rebuild their UI in a more modern way, using patterns like mircofrontends which will enabled modularity and ease to change the UI.
On top of that they wanted to make sure the UI Components are well tested and integrate with the backend in a good way that ensured the system will work 100% of the time.

They need test automation tools like Playwright and libs like Cucumber.js. 

## The Science Behind Their Approach.
To test AI Tools the team didn't just go and use Github Copilot and create frontend components and click away.
They "engineered" a way to use AI Tools (Agents, MCP Servers, etc) to make sure they get maximum value and this is what made my heart giggle.

The drafted a process what a mirco UI Component should be like, gave the AI Agent context via instruction and prompt files.
A whole engineering team sat, shared context, and by the whole team I mean technical and non-technical people were involved in the process.
